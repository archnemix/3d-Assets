of the files as they are (not only inside a compiled game), and public display in
this gallery. The caveats below are real and are not hidden by that summary.

## What CC0 does not cover

- An author's stated request is honoured even where CC0 would legally allow more. A source whose creator asks that copies not be redistributed or resold is not accepted, and any already-published asset from such a source is retired. KayKit was withdrawn on exactly this basis.
- CC0 waives copyright and related rights. It does not waive trademark or patent rights, and it does not clear rights a third party may hold in a depicted subject.
- CC0 offers no warranty. The library's own technical and visual checks are the quality guarantee, not the license.
- Nothing here is exclusive. The same files are available to everyone else under the same terms.
- A provider can change future terms, but a CC0 dedication already applied to a published work is irrevocable. Every asset folder keeps the license text and evidence URL that applied at acquisition time.
- Storefronts have their own submission rules. AI-assisted assets may need a content disclosure (for example on Steam) even though the license permits the use.
